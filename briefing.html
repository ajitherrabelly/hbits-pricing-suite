1 · Executive Summary

The New York State Office of General Services (OGS) procures Hourly-Based IT Services (HBITS) through a centralized, non-negotiated, fixed-bill-rate contract vehicle. Bidders submit Not-To-Exceed (NTE) hourly bill rates for 31 job titles across 4 skill levels and 3 regions; those rates are firm for the life of each placement.

Best Pegasus's pricing has been modeled in two versions:

  • Version 1 (Baseline) — derived from an internal cost/CPI estimation; lands in the expensive third of the field.
  • Version 1.1 (Rank Positioning) — the current formula; reverse-engineers the competitor field and prices each role to a target rank of #19 of 33 bidders (mid-field).
For the reviewer This briefing exists to be challenged. Section 11 lists the specific assumptions and design choices we believe most warrant scrutiny — starting with whether a flat mid-field rank target is compatible with the MSP's 50% price filter.

2 · Procurement Context

The vehicle

HBITS (Group 73012, Award 23158, refreshed under Solicitation 23311) is used by state agencies, local governments, school districts, public authorities, and eligible non-profits ("Authorized Users"). The contract term is five years with a two-year extension option; the bid opening date is July 1, 2026. Task orders under the prior award continue on their own terms, so the rates established at transition are decisive for incoming contractors.

The MSP price filter — the critical mechanic

When an agency issues a task order, a Managed Service Provider (MSP) collects candidate submissions from all active vendors and applies a price filter before any technical evaluation:

  • 10–40 candidates: only the lowest 50% of bill rates are forwarded (plus any candidate within 1% of the cutoff). The rest are rejected on price alone.
  • 6–10 candidates: only the 5 lowest bill rates are forwarded.
  • ≤5 candidates: the filter is bypassed — all candidates are forwarded.
Why this matters to the formula For high-volume, commoditized roles the pool routinely exceeds 10 candidates, so roughly the lowest half survive. A rate positioned at rank #19 of 33 sits in the upper half — and would be filtered out before technical review. See Section 11, item A.

3 · The Strategic Story

HBITS is engineered to be price-driven and transparent. That creates a structural tension every bidder must resolve:

  • Bid too high (large markup) → protect margin per placement, but get filtered out of high-volume task orders before anyone reads the résumé.
  • Bid too low (small markup) → pass the filter and win volume, but compress margin and starve the overhead needed to administer the work.

There is a second-order effect on talent. Because the bill rate is fixed, a low-markup vendor can pay the consultant a higher wage for the same bill rate than a high-markup competitor — recruiting better people and earning higher agency satisfaction scores. Example from the source analysis: at a Business Analyst Junior bill of $40.33, an 11% markup vendor can pay ~$36.33, while a 47% markup vendor can pay only ~$27.43.

The question the formula answers "Given a fixed, transparent, filter-driven market, where exactly should we place each of our 372 rates?" Version 1.1 answers it with a single rule — target rank #19 — which is precisely what this review should pressure-test.

4 · Core Pricing Formula

Every bill rate is the consultant wage grossed up by the vendor's markup:

Rbill = Rwage × ( 1 + M / 100 ) // HBR_NTE = HWR × (1 + markup)

The markup M must cover payroll taxes, insurance, corporate overhead & G&A, the OGS administrative fee, and net profit.

Annual CPI escalation (Section 6.5)

NTE rates may be escalated each contract anniversary by the change in the unadjusted CPI-U (Series CUUR0000SA0), using the index for the third month prior to the anniversary:

Price Adjustment (%) = ( CPIt − CPIt−12 ) / CPIt−12 × 100

Escalation is not automatic — the contractor must file the request with BLS index sheets between 90 days and the first of the month before the anniversary, or forfeit that year's adjustment.

5 · Version 1 — Baseline Formula

Version 1 takes Best Pegasus's internally estimated Bid Year 2026 bill rates (the "NYS OGS IT Vendor Rate Estimation"). These are built bottom-up from labor benchmarks and the CPI multipliers above, then presented as Region 1 averages.

  • Covers 30 of 31 titles — the source document omits Business Analyst.
  • Region 1 values are applied across all three regions in the dataset.
  • Resulting competitive position: expensive third of the field (e.g., Programmer / R1 / Senior ranks #26 of 33).
Limitation A purely cost-up baseline ignores where competitors actually sit, so it tends to price above the filter cutoff for commodity roles. This is what motivated Version 1.1.

6 · Version 1.1 — Rank Positioning Formula (current)

Version 1.1 is a market-relative rule. For each Title × Region × Skill it inspects the live competitor field and places Best Pegasus at a chosen target rank (default #19 of 33 — 18 competitors below us, 14 above).

Method

Let the 32 competitor bill rates be sorted ascending: c(1) ≤ c(2) ≤ … ≤ c(32). To land at rank 19 of 33 we need exactly 18 competitors strictly below us:

choose B* such that c(18) < B* < c(19) // 18 below, 14 above ⇒ rank 19 B* = midpoint( c(18) , c(19) ) rounded to whole cents, nudged so exactly 18 competitors are strictly below and none tie.
  • Computed independently per region (regional competitor sets differ).
  • 356 / 372 roles hit exactly #19. 16 / 372 land at #18 because the 18th and 19th cheapest competitors are tied (an exact #19 is mathematically impossible) — the nearest, more-competitive rank is used.
  • Because it only needs competitor data, it covers all 31 titles, including Business Analyst.
  • The target rank is a single tunable parameter — the Bid Builder lets you re-rank live (e.g., #10, #15) and see the impact.
Strength Self-calibrating to the actual field, region-specific, fully transparent, and reproducible from public award-notice data.

7 · Data Foundation & Provenance

  • Source: public OGS award-notice pricing schedules (Attachment 1) for each awarded contractor under Group 73012 / Award 23158.
  • Coverage: 32 competitor rate cards, each parsed to exactly 372 rows (31 titles × 4 skills × 3 regions).
  • Currency rule: each vendor's schedule contains several effective-date sections; we keep only that vendor's latest effective date — their current contracted (NTE) rate.
  • Best Pegasus: added as a 33rd bidder per combo, under whichever formula version is selected.

9 · Margin & Cost-Build Decomposition

At a market-positioned bill rate, profit depends on the cost build. Using the standard decomposition:

Net profit = (Rbill − Rwage) − Rwage×(payroll+insurance+overhead) − Rbill×OGSfee Net margin % = Net profit / Rbill × 100

Default assumptions: payroll taxes 13.5%, insurance 3.5%, overhead + G&A 10.5% (of wage); OGS fee 0.75% (of bill); markup sets the wage.

MarkupImplied wage (of bill)Net margin (approx)
30%~77%~1–2%
40%~71%~8%
45%~69%~11%
Key property Because cost loadings are a percentage of wage, net margin is driven almost entirely by the chosen markup, not by the individual role. Rank-#19 bill rates require roughly a 40–45% markup to clear a healthy (~10%) margin — which in turn lowers the wage we can offer (recruiting risk) and, for high-volume roles, may push us toward the filter cutoff.

The source analysis recommends a tiered markup: low (15–25%) for commoditized, high-supply roles (Business Analyst, Tester, Technical Writer, Graphic Designer) to pass the 50% filter, and higher (35–45%) for scarce roles (Cloud Engineer, Database/Software Architect, Security Manager) whose pools fall below 5 and bypass the filter entirely.

11 · Quality-Test Checklist — what to scrutinize

  1. Critical A. Does rank #19 survive the MSP 50% filter? In a 33-bidder, high-volume pool, only the lowest ~16–17 are forwarded. Rank #19 is in the upper half and would be filtered out before technical evaluation. Should the target rank be ≤16 for commodity roles?
  2. Critical B. Are competitor benchmarks current? Many incumbent rates are pre-2025 and un-escalated. Ranking our 2026 rate against stale rates may overstate how "expensive" we look. Should competitor rates be escalated to 2026-equivalents (via CPI) before ranking?
  3. Material C. Flat rank vs. tiered strategy. A single #19 target ignores role scarcity. The doctrine recommends low markups for high-supply roles and high markups for filter-bypassing scarce roles. Should the target rank vary by title?
  4. Material D. Margin sustainability vs. recruiting. Rank-#19 pricing yields ~1–2% margin at a 30% markup; ~10% needs ~45%. Higher markup lowers the wage we can pay (talent risk) and raises filter risk. Is the markup/wage trade-off validated against real recruiting data?
  5. Material E. Wage & cost-build assumptions. Best Pegasus's wage is currently derived at an assumed 30% markup; the cost-build percentages are illustrative. Replace with audited fringe, overhead, G&A, and fee rates.
  6. Check F. Profitability decay over 10 years. NTE rates are fixed; CPI escalation (~2.3–3.8%/yr) typically trails IT wage inflation (~3.5–5.5%/yr). Model margin erosion across the 5+2 year term, not just year one.
  7. Check G. Tie-affected roles. 16 of 372 combos land at #18 (not #19) due to competitor ties. Confirm this is acceptable.
  8. Check H. Data completeness. Are all 32 cards the full awarded pool? Any primes, resellers, or recent modifications missing?
  9. Check I. Region modeling. V1.1 computes per region (correct); V1 applied Region 1 across all regions. Confirm regional competitor sets are genuinely distinct.
  10. Check J. Escalation compliance. Is there an internal workflow to file the CPI adjustment in the 90-day window every anniversary so margin isn't silently eroded?

12 · Risks, Assumptions & Limitations

  • Assumption: competitors will re-bid at or near their current Award 23158 rates under 23311 (their 2026 bids are unknown).
  • Assumption: the MSP filter mechanics carry forward unchanged under Solicitation 23311.
  • Limitation: displayed Best Pegasus wage and margin are illustrative pending audited cost data.
  • Limitation: Version 1 omits Business Analyst (source-document gap).
  • Limitation: ranking treats each vendor's latest effective rate as current regardless of its year.
