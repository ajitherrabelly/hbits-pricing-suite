How Net Margin Is Defined

Every margin figure in this suite — the 38.5% on a KBI quote, the 11.3% on a Panha bid, the two-tier charts — comes from one transparent cost model. Here is exactly what goes into it, the formula, and a calculator you can drive yourself.

1. The short version

Net margin is the profit left after taking the wage and every loaded cost out of the bill rate, expressed as a percent of the bill:

// the bill rate is the wage marked up bill = wage × (1 + markup/100) // loaded cost = burdens on the wage + the OGS fee on the bill cost = wage × (0.135 + 0.035 + 0.105) + bill × 0.0075 // net profit per hour, then margin as a share of the bill net_profit = bill − wage − cost net_margin = net_profit / bill

2. What's in the cost build

Four loadings. Three are a percentage of the wage; one is a percentage of the bill.

Cost componentRateApplied toWhat it covers
Payroll burden13.5%wageEmployer taxes (FICA, FUTA/SUTA), workers' comp
Insurance3.5%wageHealth / benefits load
Overhead + G&A10.5%wageRecruiting, back-office, corporate overhead
OGS contract fee0.75%billNYS OGS administrative fee on the bill rate
Wage burden total27.5%wage13.5% + 3.5% + 10.5%

5. The big consequence: markup drives margin, not the role

Because every loading is a fixed percentage, the role and seniority cancel out — net margin depends almost entirely on the markup. That is why each vendor's margin is flat across all titles, and why the field splits into two tiers:

VendorMarkupNet marginTier

Why ~38.5% at a 110% markup? A 110% markup means bill = 2.10 × wage. Costs eat the wage plus 27.5% of the wage plus 0.75% of the bill, so profit ≈ (2.10 − 1 − 0.275)wage − 0.0075·bill ≈ 38–39% of the bill — independent of how large the wage is.
Where these numbers live in code: the loadings (payroll 13.5%, insurance 3.5%, overhead/G&A 10.5% of wage; OGS 0.75% of bill) are defined once as TAX/INS/OH/OGS and reused everywhere — rate-comparison.html, all-vendor-rates.html, and the margin charts — so every page reports margin the same way. Glossary — What is “wage”?

The wage is the hourly rate paid to the worker — the consultant actually doing the IT work. It's your raw cost of labor, the starting input of the price, before any of your own costs or profit are added.

TermWho it's forExample
wagepaid to the worker$38.50
loaded costwage + your burdens (payroll 13.5% + insurance 3.5% + overhead/G&A 10.5% + OGS fee)$49.70
billcharged to the customer$80.85
net profitwhat you keep$31.15
Wage is not your total cost (you also carry ~27.5% in burdens on top) and not what the customer pays (that's the bill). In the V2 model it's the real lever: markup is a fixed rule, so competitiveness and year-over-year change both come from the sourced wage — Δbill = Δwage × (1 + markup). Whoever sources the lowest wage and holds a high markup wins the margin.

What is “markup”?

The markup is the percentage added on top of the wage to get the bill rate — the gross spread between your cost of labor and your price, as a percent of the wage:

bill = wage × (1 + markup/100) markup % = (bill − wage) / wage × 100
MarkupOn wage $38.50Bill rate→ Net margin
45% (Panha)× 1.45$55.8211.3%
100% (Genesys)× 2.00$77.0035.5%
110% (KBI / BP V2)× 2.10$80.8538.5%
Markup ≠ margin. Markup is measured against the wage (your cost): “$80.85 is 110% above the $38.50 wage.” Net margin is measured against the bill (your price), after loaded costs: “$31.15 ÷ $80.85 = 38.5%.” Same deal, two denominators — and because every cost loading is a fixed %, the markup alone decides the margin tier.
