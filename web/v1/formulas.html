How Our Two Pricing Formulas Work

No jargon, no math degree needed. In the next two minutes you'll understand exactly how Best Pegasus sets its prices — using a simple lemonade-stand story that anyone can follow.

Step 1 · The basics in 30 seconds

🍋 Think of a street full of lemonade stands

New York State is the customer. It needs IT workers, and lots of companies (vendors) compete to supply them — like 33 lemonade stands on one street, all selling the same cup of lemonade.

The “price” is the hourly bill rate — what the State pays per hour for that worker. Every company sets its own price. Out of that price, the company pays the worker a wage and keeps the rest to cover its costs and profit.

🧠 The one rule that changes everything: when many companies offer a worker, the State usually only looks at the cheaper half of the prices first. Price too high and you don't even get a look. Price too low and you barely make any money. So where you set your price matters enormously.
Step 2 · The two ways we can pick our price
Formula 1.0

🧮 The “Recipe” method

“Set my price from my own costs — without looking next door.”

We add up what it costs us (the worker's wage, taxes, overhead) and add a fair profit on top. That becomes our price. We don't look at what the other stands are charging.

🍋 Like pricing your lemonade purely on what your lemons and sugar cost you — even if the stand next door is selling the same cup for much less.
  • Simple and fair to our own books
  • Guarantees we cover our costs
  • We often end up the most expensive on the street
  • So the State may skip us before even tasting it
Formula 1.1

📍 The “Line-Up” method

“Look at everyone's price, then pick exactly where to stand.”

We line up all the other stands from cheapest to priciest, then deliberately place our price at a chosen spot — the 19th cheapest out of 33. Never the cheapest (we'd make too little), never the priciest (we'd be ignored).

🍋 Like walking the whole street first, seeing every price, then setting yours so you're comfortably in the pack — visible to buyers but still making a healthy profit.
  • Always priced relative to the real competition
  • Protects profit while staying in the game
  • Works for every role automatically
  • #19 is just above the middle — for very busy roles we may aim a bit cheaper
Step 3 · See it with real (tiny) numbers

Same role, two different prices

Say five rival stands charge these hourly prices for the same worker: $61, $66, $67, … up to $85. Here's where each formula would put us:

Same worker, same market — we simply chose a smarter spot to stand. That small move from “26th” to “19th” is the whole idea behind Formula 1.1.

The bottom line

Formula 1.0 prices from our own recipe and can leave us looking expensive. Formula 1.1 prices by looking at the whole street and choosing our spot on purpose — so we stay competitive and profitable.
